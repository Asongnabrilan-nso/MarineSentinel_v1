Problem Statement

Stage 1

Who has the problem

Municipal water authorities, fishing communities, and environmental NGOs on the Wouri estuary and Cameroon's coastal and inland water bodies, who currently have no continuous measurement of either plastic pollution or water quality.

What exactly is the problem

Plastic debris and water quality anomalies are recorded, when they are recorded at all, by someone physically travelling to a site and taking a sample or a photograph. Between visits the water is unobserved, so a pollution event that lasts a day is indistinguishable from one that lasts a month, and neither is attributable to a source.

Why it matters

Commercial continuous monitoring is priced out of reach: a YSI EXO2 sonde on a NexSens CB-450 hull is roughly 25,000-40,000 USD before freight, and lands in Cameroon at 1.6-1.9x that after CEMAC duty, 19.25% VAT and clearing. At 24,000,000-42,000,000 XAF per station, no Cameroonian municipality will instrument a river. The result is that the water bodies with the worst plastic loading have the least data about it, and 'we have no numbers' is the standing reason funding proposals fail.

Why hardware

The measurement is physical and continuous. Turbidity, temperature, wave state and floating debris exist at a specific point on the water at a specific moment; nothing can be inferred about them from a phone, a satellite image at 10m resolution, or a survey form. It requires a sensor package that stays on the water when nobody is there.

OpenCTD - Oceanography for Everyone
MIT-licensed open-source conductivity/temperature/depth instrument for citizen scientists, sub-500 USD, peer-reviewed in Oceanography (TOS).		The most complete open release in this space: construction manual, firmware, printable parts, control PCB and calibration software published as one package. Academically citable.It is a hand-cast profiler, not a moored platform - no telemetry, no solar, no GPS, no IMU, no camera, no dashboard. Data comes off an SD card by hand, so it cannot detect an event, only record one for later. Pressure housing relies on epoxy potting, a hand skill with a real first-timer failure rate.Steal the release model - manual plus calibration software plus BOM as a single downloadable package - and steal the epoxy-potting technique for the turbidity and temperature probes. Reject the retrieve-to-read data model; that gap is exactly what MarineSentinel fills.
Smart-Buoy (Adam Whitehouse, Hackaday.io)
Solar-powered DIY buoy measuring wave height and period from an IMU, plus water and air temperature, pressure and GPS, radioing to a Raspberry Pi Zero base station.		The closest existing architectural sibling. Derives wave state from the IMU, which is precisely the MPU6050 fusion MarineSentinel needs, and logs its own battery voltage and current so the power budget is empirical rather than guessed.Ships as two STL files with no parametric CAD source, so the hull cannot be resized for a different battery or a camera port without remodelling from scratch. NRF24L01 is the only link (~1 km line-of-sight at best). The SD logger was removed because of a library flash conflict, so any radio dropout is permanent data loss. Effectively abandoned for ~7 years.Steal the IMU-to-wave-state processing and the self-monitoring power telemetry. Avoid STL-only release and avoid a single lossy radio link with no local logging - MarineSentinel keeps a microSD store-and-forward log from v0.1.
Open-source LoRaWAN aquaculture buoy (Sarmiento et al., PLOS ONE 2022)
Peer-reviewed, fully open (schematics, PCB, firmware) STM32L476 + RFM95W water quality buoy, ~658 USD, field-trialled 7 days near Neiva, Colombia.		The best cost transparency available: it publishes that ~85% of build cost is the sensor probes, which is the single most useful budgeting fact for this project. Runs on three AA cells. Flotation is a 10 L expanded polystyrene box plus an IP55 enclosure and PG13.5 glands - buildable in Douala from market materials.No camera, no GPS, no IMU - a static moored point sensor. LoRaWAN depends on gateway coverage, and the paper documents a gateway failing near an aluminium building. Biofouling is explicitly listed as unaddressed future work. Only 7 days of field data, so no drift evidence.Steal the 85%-is-sensors budgeting rule and the EPS-box plus IP55-gland flotation approach. Avoid pricing the design around Atlas Scientific probes, which do not survive Cameroonian import markup, and do not defer biofouling - plan a wiper or a replaceable window from day one.
UnifiedWater (Andrei Florian)
Open-source cellular water quality monitor on an Arduino MKR GSM 1400 with pH, turbidity and temperature, streaming to an Azure IoT Central dashboard.		The only open build that pairs turbidity, cellular telemetry and a real dashboard end to end - the same data path MarineSentinel needs. The two-mode design (fixed fleet plus portable spot survey) is a reusable product framing.Locked to Azure IoT Central: proprietary backend, recurring cost, and an account dependency that kills the project if funding lapses. Hologram SIM assumptions do not map onto MTN or Orange Cameroon. The author documents that early versions were not properly waterproof. Turbidity output is the cheap uncalibrated IR module with no NTU traceability.Steal the turbidity-plus-dashboard data path and the portable-survey mode. Avoid cloud lock-in entirely - MarineSentinel serves its own dashboard from the UNO Q, so it works with no internet at all, which is the only assumption that holds on a Cameroonian river.
YSI EXO2 multiparameter sonde (Xylem)
The industry-reference 7-port water quality sonde for unattended long-term deployment - the instrument MarineSentinel will be benchmarked against.		A central wiper mechanically cleans the optical ports, which solves the exact failure that kills every DIY turbidity sensor. Calibration coefficients are stored on each smart sensor, so field swaps need no recalibration. Years of unattended deployment records.Roughly 20,000-30,000 USD configured, landing in Cameroon at an estimated 32,000-55,000 USD after duty, VAT, freight and clearing - so it is usually rented rather than owned. Fully proprietary and closed-protocol, with no user-serviceable parts and no service path in Central Africa. Calibration standards are consumables with expiry dates and shipping restrictions. No camera and no debris detection of any kind.Steal two ideas cheaply: a hobby servo with a silicone blade as a wiper over the turbidity window, and storing calibration constants on the probe assembly so it is hot-swappable. Position MarineSentinel explicitly as most of the signal at a small fraction of the landed cost - and note that even the gold standard cannot see a plastic bottle.
RanMarine WasteShark / WasteShark Pro
Autonomous electric catamaran aqua-drone that skims floating plastic while logging water quality; the Pro edition runs an NVIDIA Jetson with LiDAR and waypoint navigation over 4G.		Commercial proof that onboard AI vision on water works. Publishes a concrete performance envelope - 78 kg, 200 mm draft, 8 h runtime, ~10 km range, up to 500 kg/day over 5,000 m2/day - which makes a clean comparison table.Price is unpublished (reported 15,000-25,000+ EUR base, materially more for Pro), which is itself a barrier. It is a collection robot, not a monitoring station: 8 hours of endurance means daily human recovery and charging, so it is a labour model rather than an autonomous sentinel. Jetson-class compute is power-hungry and expensive; 78 kg needs a ramp and a crew.Steal the always-connected edge-AI architecture and use their envelope as the comparison baseline. Avoid propulsion and Jetson-class compute - MarineSentinel's defensible niche is being a passive, permanently moored, milliwatt-class detector that tells a cleanup crew where to go, not a machine that competes with them.
Real-time monitoring of water quality dynamics using low-cost sensor networks in Lagos Lagoon (Cambridge Prisms: Water, 2025)
Raspberry Pi 3 plus DS18B20, camera module and hydrophone deployed in Lagos Lagoon in December 2024, with an ML classifier over 31 species.		The most contextually relevant work available - West African brackish tropical lagoon, camera plus sensors plus ML, same climate and same infrastructure constraints, published recently. Gives real design numbers: water temperature 28.5-31.5 C, so thermal margins are set from tropical data rather than European ranges.Read the failure list as a design brief. Wi-Fi died after roughly 10 cm of submersion, so the antenna must sit well above the waterline. High turbidity produced dark, low-detail images and degraded the vision model. Battery runtime was only ~6 hours on a Pi 3. Waterproofing took three design iterations. The deployment lasted ~2 days, so there is no biofouling or drift evidence.Adopt every one of its failure modes as a MarineSentinel requirement, and cite it as the regional justification for the project. Avoid Pi-class compute for a battery-powered node, and never assume a radio works near the waterline.
Real-Time Detection of Floating Plastics in a Stream Using Video Cameras and Deep Learning (Sensors, 2025)
A camera on a bridge at 15 degrees to the water surface, 1080p/30fps over 90 minutes after heavy rain, running YOLOv8-nano plus Deep-SORT over plastics, bottles, film and fragments.		Effectively a feasibility study for MarineSentinel's camera pipeline, with hard numbers: 92.34% classification accuracy, mAP@0.5 of 0.990 on test, F1 0.980, using a nano-scale model chosen explicitly for edge portability. The 15-degree geometry and post-rainfall timing are directly copyable.The tracking result is the warning: only 6 of 32 tracked items crossed the counting line, because objects submerge and re-emerge and break track identity. Counting is far harder than detection. mAP@0.5:0.95 falls to 0.714, and it is a single location on a single rainfall event, from a static bridge mount that never pitches or rolls.Steal the 15-degree downward geometry, the four-class taxonomy, and the accuracy figures as a benchmark. Avoid promising object counts in v0.1 - report detection events and a debris density index instead, and use the IMU to gate frames captured while the capsule is level, which is a genuine novelty a bridge mount never needed.
TU Delft Green Village floating litter dataset (Frontiers in Water, 2023)
An open dataset of 9,473 water-surface images labelled into four litter-density classes (none / 1-2 / 3-5 / 6-10), captured from fixed cameras at 2.7 m and 4.0 m height and 0 and 45 degree angles.		The most directly reusable training data for this project, and it explicitly benchmarks MobileNetV2 against DenseNet121 (91.7%), SqueezeNet (89.6%), ResNet50 and InceptionV3 - direct evidence for the Edge Impulse architecture choice. Its density-class framing sidesteps the counting problem that broke the Sensors 2025 tracker.A temperate Dutch canal: no tropical water colour, no water hyacinth, no high-turbidity brown water, no tropical glare, so expect a real domain gap. Classification labels only, no bounding boxes. Saturates at 10 items per frame. Licence must be confirmed on the Zenodo record before redistribution.Adopt the density-class label scheme as MarineSentinel's output schema - it is honest about what a small model can do and more useful to an NGO than a wrong exact count. Fine-tune on locally captured Douala frames; that local set is the fix for the domain gap and the project's most citable original contribution.
PositioningNothing in the reviewed field combines edge-AI debris detection, water quality sensing and IMU-derived sea state on a low-cost moored buoy. The commercial AI platforms (WasteShark Pro, Clearbot) are expensive collection robots that need a crew and a charger every eight hours; the open-source buoys (Smart-Buoy, the PLOS aquaculture buoy, Smart Citizen) have no camera at all; and the floating-plastic vision papers all run on bridge-mounted or drone cameras that never pitch or roll. MarineSentinel occupies that intersection as a passive always-on sensor rather than a robot: it does not collect debris, it tells a cleanup crew where and when debris is flowing, self-hosts its own dashboard so it needs no cloud subscription and no internet, and publishes parametric CAD and an openly licensed locally-captured dataset so a workshop in Bamenda or Lagos can rebuild it. If a single sentence has to carry it: this is the sensor layer for somebody else's cleanup, at roughly one thousandth of the landed cost of the instrument it is benchmarked against.

Boundary conditions — what this project does NOT do
  • Does not detect debris underwater or below the surface - the camera is above-water and surface-looking only.
  • Does not count individual debris items or estimate flux. Output is a per-frame density class plus detection events, per the Sensors 2025 tracking finding.
  • Does not measure pH, dissolved oxygen or TDS in v0.1. Electrochemical probes need consumable calibration standards that cannot be sourced locally, and nothing in this design is imported.
  • Turbidity is reported as an uncalibrated relative index, never in NTU. The sensing cell is self-built from local parts and has no traceable calibration standard behind it.
  • No cellular or LoRa telemetry, no cloud backend and no remote access in v0.1 - the dashboard is reachable only over the device's own Wi-Fi access point, within radio range of the capsule.
  • No solar charging and no long-endurance power management. The prototype runs from a USB power bank, which bounds every outdoor session to a few hours.
  • Outdoor testing is a tethered or hand-recovered float on an accessible open water body. There is no mooring, no station-keeping, no unattended overnight deployment and no recovery plan for a capsule that drifts out of reach - a retrieval line is mandatory.
  • No anti-fouling wiper in v0.1 - the camera window is designed to be replaceable instead.
  • GPS provides position and fix quality only. There is no navigation, no geofencing and no track-following behaviour in v0.1.

Success criteria

  • A phone connected to the capsule's Wi-Fi access point, standing at the water's edge, shows live video, a live debris classification, live turbidity and temperature, a live GPS position, and a 3D capsule that visibly tilts when the real capsule is tilted by passing waves.
  • The classifier reaches at least 80% accuracy on a held-out set of locally captured frames covering the three classes (open water / plastic bottle / other debris), with the held-out set drawn from the same outdoor water body and lighting conditions as the demo.
  • The pipeline sustains >= 2 FPS for 30 continuous minutes outdoors with no crash and no thermal throttle.
  • The sealed capsule floats upright for 30 minutes in open water with no ingress on the tissue witness.
  • A CSV log recovered from the microSD card contains a continuous, timestamped record of the entire 30-minute outdoor run, including a GPS fix on every row where one was held.
  • A 60-90 second demo video shows the capsule on open water with the dashboard visible and readable, suitable for publication alongside the 29 August announcement.
  • Every requirement above has a matching test ID in docs/test_plan.md, and every test has a recorded PASS or FAIL.

Critical pathThe chain is now SW-01 into SW-02 into SW-03 into SW-04 into TEST-03 into MEDIA-01 into MEDIA-02. CAD and the printed capsule are done, which takes two days of mechanical risk off the front of the sprint, but it does not shorten this chain - it only means the chain now starts on day 1 instead of waiting. The binding constraint is SW-01: collecting 600 frames outdoors at three times of day at the real test site. Every day that image collection slips is a day the dashboard, the float test and the video all slip, and the video cannot be re-shot after the fact because it has to be cut from the same session as the float test.
This schedule assumes
  • The Arduino UNO Q, a working 3D printer and a laptop are all in hand and functioning. The UNO Q has no local supplier and nothing is imported, so its failure ends the sprint outright.
  • The CAD, the five STL exports and the printed, assembled capsule are complete as of 18 August. This is the reason 11 remaining days is workable at all.
  • Everything in the BOM is actually in stock in Douala or Yaounde. Nothing is imported, so a part the market does not have is a part that gets substituted the same day - not one that gets waited for.
  • An open water body suitable for a tethered 30-minute float test is reachable within a short trip, and reachable more than once - image collection (SW-01), the float test (TEST-03) and the video shoot (MEDIA-01) all need to happen there.
  • Effectively full-time work on all remaining days, including the weekends of 22-23 and 29 August. There is no slack day anywhere in this plan.
  • Edge Impulse Studio is reachable on a usable connection for model training. Training is the one step in the sprint that genuinely needs the internet; the device itself never does.
  • Outdoor testing means weather. A day lost to heavy rain is a day lost from a plan with no slack, so the float test is scheduled early enough that one weather day can be absorbed before 29 August.
